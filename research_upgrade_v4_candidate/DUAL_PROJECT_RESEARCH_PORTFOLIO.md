# WaterSense AI + AquaVision AI — Research Portfolio

These projects investigate a shared question: how much evidence is required before a machine-learning result can be called reliable outside its development data?

**WaterSense AI** studies dissolved-oxygen prediction from 151,031 observations at 840 Northern Ireland monitoring stations. Its design moves beyond random rows to station-grouped, temporal, spatiotemporal, basin, and nested validation. The strongest V3 development result reduced Low-DO MAE from 3.3235 to 2.3613 mg/L and increased recall from 0.1293 to 0.3641, but overall MAE and false positives worsened. A V4 classifier evaluated under a different development protocol recorded 0.9222 recall, 0.0684 precision and 9,517 false positives at 0.6333% prevalence. The V3/V4 values are descriptive and not directly comparable as a performance-improvement experiment. Hypothetical cost sensitivity shows that preference depends on stated assumptions. The checked official release is byte-identical to the historical source; this source is not independent, while broader external validation remains incomplete.

**AquaVision AI** studies Low, Moderate, and High CyAN-derived bloom-proxy segmentation from twelve-band Sentinel-2 imagery. A historical Logistic Regression Test High F1 of 0.5803 was not used to overturn the validation-selected NDCI model, because doing so would select on Test. The deeper limitation is geographic: the 288 historical pairs do not retain enough identity evidence to prove independent lakes. A later pilot successfully linked six Sentinel scenes to named waterbodies, footprints, dates, and hash-verified CyAN V6 products, but those scenes represent only three waterbody histories and none passed the frozen independent High-support gate. V4 therefore stopped model training and retained `NOT READY`.

The strongest combined contribution is methodological judgment: grouping by the real unit of generalization, keeping preprocessing inside training folds, freezing selection rules before evaluation, preserving negative findings, and refusing to convert technical reproducibility into external scientific confirmation. The most important combined failure is lack of genuinely independent confirmation data.

The applicant states that they were the sole human project lead and used substantial AI assistance for implementation, statistical analysis, testing, auditing, and drafting. This is a self-reported contribution statement, not independent certification. Before submission, the applicant should personally verify every contribution claim and be able to explain the split logic, metrics, failure cases, and stop decisions. Neither project claims publication, professor endorsement, operational deployment, or independent scientific confirmation.
